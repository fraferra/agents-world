A FIELD GUIDE

Watch a society become.

Common Ground is an artificial-life experiment. Each individual weighs needs, values, beliefs, experience, and opportunities. Their plans change as the world changes. Collective patterns emerge from those local choices.

A life of their own

Survival comes first. When safe, people weigh research, work, learning, care, and exploration through their temperament, feelings, memories, experience, and life aspirations. They learn techniques and pass them on, and they remember places and who can be trusted. Inspect a person to see their thoughts and the reasons behind a decision.

Belief and belonging

People form and share invented traditions, including spiritual beliefs. Their doctrines can encourage solidarity, learning, exchange, authority, or militancy. Societies form trading ties and alliances; scarcity, grievances, and incompatible aims can also lead to war.

The next generation

Adults form pairs and have children when circumstances allow. Traits are inherited with variation. Children learn skills and ideas through encounters and teaching. People age and die; living people pass on knowledge while the world retains a historical record of discoveries.

A changing world

Regions differ in food, forests, fertility, stone, ore, clay, fiber, herbs, game, fish and gems. Thirty technologies, from stone tools to the steam engine, aviation, computers and nuclear weapons, and twenty-seven buildings depend on local materials, including finite coal and uranium. Industry reshapes the societies that adopt it: machines multiply labor and concentrate wealth, coal smoke sickens, hospitals save children, and nuclear arsenals deter or destroy. From World conditions you can perform acts of god: winters, plagues, festivals, omens, inspiration, wanderers, earthquakes, fires, floods and mineral strikes.

Invention is an experiment

People combine materials, methods, principles, and earlier ideas into new designs. Tests consume supplies and effort, can fail, and produce different benefits and tradeoffs. Foundations shows the nine basic techniques; Inventions follows generated designs and their lineages.

Cultures that grow

Societies develop norms and customs from what they eat, make and live through, choose leaders, rise from bands to cities, and send willing pioneers to found colonies. The Cultures tab follows each society's character, customs, lineage and land pressure. Industry shows production chains, stocks and diet.

Keep an experiment going

The world autosaves in this browser every minute and whenever you leave the tab. Export a file to keep a permanent checkpoint. Browser simulation pauses when the tab is suspended or closed; resume from your last save, or use the included headless runner for uninterrupted experiments.

One year = 120 simulation days. There is no numerical population ceiling: finite resources and their renewal constrain growth, while technology changes productivity. Agents use a utility policy, memory, and symbolic communication. New designs and beliefs come from a generative compositional model with fixed physical and social primitives, variable outcomes, and inherited ideas. Voices renders recorded exchanges as templates. This is an artificial-life model, not a language model or a prediction of human society.
